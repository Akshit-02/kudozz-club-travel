VARIANT-BASED TEMPLATE — read by scripts/send-campaign.js
===========================================================
For every send, the script randomly picks ONE numbered line from each of
the four sections below (SUBJECT, FIRST LINE, SECOND LINE, BLOG LINE),
fills in {{FIRST_NAME}} / {{DESTINATION}} / {{DESTINATION_URL}}, and
assembles them into the fixed skeleton shown at the bottom of this file.
That's 5 x 5 x 5 x 5 = 625 wordings per destination — combined with the
10-way random destination pick in send-campaign.js, no two recipients are
likely to receive an identical email. This is deliberate: identical bulk
content is itself a spam-filter signal, on top of everything else in the
deliverability notes below.

Add, remove, or reword lines freely — just keep each one on a single line
starting with its label ("SUBJECT 3:", "BLOG LINE 2:", etc.). The count
doesn't have to stay at exactly 5 per section.

DELIVERABILITY NOTES (reference only — not parsed by the script)
-----------------------------------------------------------
- All 625 combinations avoid "free," "checklist," and other bulk-mail-flagged
  words in favor of specific, concrete language.
- Every BLOG LINE variant uses the full https:// URL, never a bare domain
  or shortener.
- No urgency language ("act now," "limited time," etc.) in any variant —
  filters weigh that heavily regardless of which line gets picked.
- Before sending at volume: confirm SPF, DKIM, and DMARC are configured on
  the sending domain, warm up gradually (see README), and send from a real
  person's address (e.g. arjun@kudozz.in), not newsletter@ or noreply@.
- By request, the assembled email ends at the "Kudozz Club" sign-off — no
  postal address or unsubscribe link. Worth knowing: CAN-SPAM (US) and
  similar laws elsewhere require both in commercial email, so this is a
  compliance trade-off, not just a style choice. The other plain-text
  templates in this kit (08, 10, 11) still include that footer.
